### 1. 【The LEGO Assembly Line】
**Question:** What type of bond links amino acids together to form a protein? Which amino acids form hydrogen bonds with each other?

**Answer:**  
Amino acids are linked together to form a protein by peptide bonds (a type of covalent amide bond) formed through a condensation or dehydration reaction between the carboxyl group of one amino acid and the amino group of the next.

Hydrogen bonds can form between any amino acids via their polypeptide backbone atoms (specifically between the carbonyl oxygen and the amide hydrogen). Additionally, amino acids with polar or charged side chains—such as serine, threonine, tyrosine, asparagine, glutamine, lysine, arginine, histidine, aspartic acid, and glutamic acid—can form hydrogen bonds with one another through their side chains.

### 2. 【The Double-Track Highway of Life’s Blueprint】
**Question:** How do nucleotides assemble to form double-stranded DNA? What are the basic structural components of DNA? Is DNA right-handed or left-handed? List the names of the four DNA bases. Which bases form hydrogen bonds with each other?

**Answer:**  
* **Components & Structure:** Deoxyribose sugar, phosphate group, and a nitrogenous base; they link together via phosphodiester bonds into two antiparallel strands that twist into a double helix. 
* **Handedness:** Right-handed.
* **Bases:** Adenine, thymine, cytosine, and guanine.
* **Base Pairing:** Adenine pairs with thymine, and cytosine pairs with guanine.

### 4. 【Return to the Crime Scene】
**Question:** Why can a mutation (change) in just one amino acid of a protein sometimes lead to a serious human disease? Give one specific example of such a disease.

**Answer:**  
Proteins rely on a precise sequence of amino acids to fold into a specific three-dimensional shape, which is essential for their biological function. Changing even a single amino acid can disrupt this structure, alter protein stability, or destroy an active binding site. When a protein changes shape or fails to function, it can disrupt crucial cellular processes and lead to disease.

* **The Mutation:** In the gene that encodes the hemoglobin protein, a single DNA change causes glutamic acid (a hydrophilic, water-loving amino acid) to be replaced by valine (a hydrophobic, water-fearing amino acid) at the sixth position of the protein's beta-globin chain.
* **The Result:** Because valine is hydrophobic, it causes hemoglobin molecules to stick together and form long, rigid fibers when oxygen levels are low. These fibers distort red blood cells from their normal round disk shape into a stiff, crescent "sickle" shape, leading to blocked blood vessels, organ damage, and severe anemia.
